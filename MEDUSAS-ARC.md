ACT I — THE PRIESTESS AND THE GODDESS
1. Medusa’s Devotion
Medusa serves as a priestess in Athena’s temple — disciplined, pure, and deeply loyal.
Her beauty draws attention, but her devotion is what defines her. She keeps her vows with pride, believing Athena sees her.

A young man visits the temple often, drawn to her.
There is a spark — gentle, unspoken — but Medusa chooses her vows over desire.
This choice becomes the emotional seed of the tragedy.

2. Divine Pride Awakens
Whispers spread through the city:

“She’s as beautiful as Athena herself.”

These comparisons reach the goddess.
A mortal rival, even unintentionally, is an insult to divine pride.

3. The Violation
Poseidon forces himself upon Medusa inside Athena’s sacred temple.
The act is brutal, violating both Medusa and the goddess’s sanctum.

Medusa is left shattered, ashamed, and terrified — believing she has failed Athena.

4. Athena’s Wrath
Athena appears in a blaze of divine fury.
Her anger is not directed at Medusa, but at:

the desecration of her temple

the shattering of sacred purity

the mortal comparisons

the insult to her divine pride

But Medusa, broken and kneeling, becomes the focus of the moment.

5. The Curse
Athena curses Medusa:

hair twisting into serpents

eyes becoming lethal

form becoming monstrous

But Medusa cannot perceive it.
Her psyche shields her from the horror.

The audience sees only her shadow twisting on the temple wall — a cinematic, symbolic reveal.

Medusa flees into the night.

ACT II — THE MONSTER WHO STILL THINKS SHE’S HUMAN
6. The First Death
A soldier finds Medusa collapsed on the road and tries to help her.
He turns to stone instantly.

Medusa screams, believing he simply fell dead.
She cannot see the petrification.

The audience sees the truth.
She does not.

7. The World Reacts
Rumours spread of a monster in the hills.
Armies hunt her.
Villagers flee at the sight of her silhouette.

Medusa hides in a cave, believing she is cursed with misfortune — not monstrosity.

8. The Cave of Statues
Inside the cave, Medusa wanders among the stone figures of those she has accidentally killed.

She touches them, apologises, mourns them — believing they are corpses.

The audience sees stone.
She sees bodies.

This is where the tragedy deepens.

9. The Lover Returns
The young man from her past — the one she denied for her vows — finds her cave.

He sees the monster.
But he also sees the woman inside.

He speaks softly, avoiding her gaze.
Medusa believes he is afraid of her grief, not her form.

10. The Tragic Embrace
Medusa runs to him, desperate for comfort.

He turns to stone in her arms.

This is the moment her psyche shatters.
The illusion breaks.
For the first time, the audience sees the full lamia form — monstrous, tragic, divine.

Medusa collapses, screaming, finally understanding what she has become.

This is the emotional centre of the film.

ACT III — THE HERO AND THE GODDESS
11. Perseus Arrives
Perseus enters the cave expecting a monster.
Instead, he finds:

a broken woman

surrounded by statues

weeping over the lover she killed

trapped in a curse she never deserved

He sees the tragedy, not the threat.

12. The Mercy Killing
Medusa does not beg for death.
She moves toward Perseus because he resembles the man she loved — a final, tragic reflex.

In that moment, Perseus sees her humanity.
He kills her gently, respectfully — not as a hero, but as a liberator.

13. The Altar
Perseus brings her head to Athena’s temple.
He lays it on the altar, not as a trophy, but as an offering of peace.

He asks nothing in return.

14. The Astral Purification
Athena appears.
She sees:

the truth of Medusa’s suffering

the consequences of her own pride

the tragedy she helped create

She touches the head.
It dissolves into astral light, freeing Medusa’s soul.

15. The Shield
Athena shapes the light into a shield.

In shadow:

the monstrous Gorgon face

In light:

Medusa’s human face beneath it

A dual symbol of:

protection

tragedy

remembrance

This is the film’s iconic image.

16. Final Image
Athena stands alone in the temple, holding the shield.

She does not apologise.
Gods do not apologise.

But she honours Medusa.
And she learns.

Fade out.
